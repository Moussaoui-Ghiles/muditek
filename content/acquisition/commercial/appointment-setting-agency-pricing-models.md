## Four common pricing structures

Appointment-setting agencies can charge a retainer, a fee per booked meeting, a fee per qualified held meeting, or a hybrid of operating cost and delivery fees. Some also charge setup or require separate data and software purchases.

The model name does not tell you the total price or the acceptance rule.

## Retainer

A retainer pays for capacity and work during a period. Ask which roles, channels, volumes, data, software, and reports are included. Calculate the effective cost per qualified held meeting after the period ends.

`effective cost per qualified held meeting = all period costs ÷ qualified held meetings`

## Per booked meeting

This model charges when a prospect accepts a calendar event. The buyer carries attendance risk. The contract must define duplicates, reschedules, buyer cancellations, and meetings that do not clear the target rule.

## Per qualified held meeting

This model charges after the prospect attends and clears the agreed qualification rule. The provider carries more risk through reply handling, screening, booking, and attendance.

The definition must state company fit, attendee role, relevance, exclusions, minimum attendance, no-show treatment, and dispute evidence.

## Hybrid

A hybrid separates operating cost from delivery. The recurring operating charge can fund domains, inboxes, data, enrichment, verification, sending software, and reporting. The delivery fee applies to the agreed meeting event.

The quote should state which costs are pass-through, which assets the buyer owns, and what remains after cancellation.

## Compare with one worksheet

For each quote, record setup, recurring cost, required extras, billing unit, meeting definition, no-show rule, contract term, cancellation, channels, and ownership.

Then run a low, expected, and high case using the buyer's own expected meeting count and close economics. Do not insert an industry average when a buyer input is unknown.
