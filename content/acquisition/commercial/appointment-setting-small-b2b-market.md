## Small markets need runway control

A narrow B2B market can support appointment setting, but it cannot support careless volume. Repeated contact to the same small set of accounts damages the market before the message can improve.

Start with eligible accounts, not a desired send count.

## Calculate reachable market

`eligible accounts = total candidate accounts - exclusions - customers - open opportunities - recent contacts`

Then measure buyer-path coverage:

`contactable accounts = eligible accounts with at least one verified path to an approved buyer role`

Do not count invented email permutations as verified paths.

## Calculate simple runway

Choose the number of new accounts activated per week.

`runway in weeks = remaining contactable accounts ÷ new accounts activated per week`

If 240 contactable accounts remain and 30 new accounts enter outreach each week, simple runway is eight weeks. That is arithmetic, not a performance forecast. Replies, exclusions, data decay, and new accounts can change the live number.

## Use account-level contact rules

Set a maximum number of buyer paths, messages, and active days per account. Coordinate email, LinkedIn, and phone activity around the account instead of letting each channel contact the same people independently.

Suppress the full account when a current opportunity, customer, legal restriction, or explicit stop request requires it.

## Learn without consuming the market

Use small fixed cohorts. Hold the segment, offer, list source, and observation window stable. Review qualified contacts reached, replies, positive replies, qualified conversations, meetings booked, and meetings held.

Change the first failing stage. Do not use the remaining market to test cosmetic copy variants while contactability or qualification is unknown.

## Choose a different motion when needed

If the market is too small for continuous activation, use named-account research, partner introductions, events, direct mail, or founder-led outreach. These motions can spend more attention per account without pretending the market is large.
