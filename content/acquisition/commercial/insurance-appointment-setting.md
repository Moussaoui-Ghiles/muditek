## Define the insurance market first

Insurance appointment setting can target employers, brokers, agencies, carriers, or consumers. Each market has different buyer roles, data, consent, licensing, and communication requirements.

This page addresses B2B outreach. It does not provide legal advice or replace a compliance review for the jurisdictions and products involved.

## Build the market rule

Write the account type, geography, employee or revenue range where relevant, line of insurance, renewal window if lawfully known, buyer roles, and exclusions.

Do not label an account as interested because a public fact suggests change. A hiring event, office move, ownership change, or filing can set research priority. The reply must establish relevance.

## Build the compliance gate

Before launch, the buyer should approve:

- the jurisdictions that may be contacted;
- the products and claims permitted in outreach;
- the people or entities that may discuss or solicit the product;
- the lawful source and permitted use of contact data;
- suppression, opt-out, and do-not-contact rules;
- recording and consent rules for calls;
- required disclosures and record retention;
- the escalation path for questions that require a licensed person.

The appointment setter should not answer regulated product questions outside the approved role. Route those questions to the licensed buyer-side owner.

## Define a qualified held meeting

A B2B insurance meeting rule can include the approved company type and geography, an approved attendee role, relevance to the named insurance category, minimum attendance, and exclusions for current customers or active opportunities.

Do not require the prospect to disclose protected or unnecessary personal information to make the meeting billable.

## Audit the provider

Ask who supplies data, which channels are used, how consent and suppression are managed, where records are stored, which jurisdictions are supported, and how the provider prevents unapproved claims.

Confirm the current requirements with qualified counsel and the relevant regulator before launch. The [NAIC state insurance department directory](https://content.naic.org/state-insurance-departments) links to state regulators in the United States.
