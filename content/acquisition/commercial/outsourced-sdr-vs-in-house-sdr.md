## Compare systems, not salaries

An in-house SDR is an employee inside your sales team. An outsourced SDR service supplies some or all of the research, data, outreach, reply handling, and booking work.

The real comparison is not salary versus agency fee. It is the total cost and usable capacity of each operating system.

## Build the in-house cost

Use your own numbers:

`monthly in-house cost = salary + employer costs + management time + data + software + domains and inboxes + recruiting and training allocation`

Then measure qualified meetings held using a written acceptance rule.

`in-house cost per qualified held meeting = monthly in-house cost ÷ qualified held meetings`

Do not treat activity volume as output. Calls made, emails sent, and contacts added are inputs.

## Build the outsourced cost

Include setup, recurring operating charges, retainers, per-meeting charges, and any required data or software.

`outsourced monthly cost = allocated setup + recurring charges + outcome charges`

`outsourced cost per qualified held meeting = outsourced monthly cost ÷ qualified held meetings`

Use the same qualification and attendance rules for both options.

## Decide who should own each job

| Work | In-house default | Outsourced default |
|---|---|---|
| Offer and positioning | Buyer | Buyer |
| Market approval | Buyer | Shared |
| Account and contact research | Team | Provider |
| Infrastructure | Team | Provider or shared |
| Outreach and follow-up | SDR | Provider |
| Reply screening | SDR | Provider |
| Sales call and proposal | AE or founder | Buyer |
| CRM disposition and revenue | Buyer | Buyer |

An outsourced service cannot fix slow sales follow-up, weak discovery, or missing proposal capacity.

## Choose in-house when

Choose in-house when the market requires deep product knowledge, rapid coordination with account executives, or daily learning that must stay inside the company. It also fits when there is enough durable volume to support a full role and a manager can coach it.

## Choose outsourced when

Choose outsourced when the company wants to test a defined market without first hiring and assembling the full data and sending stack. Keep ownership of domains, data, messaging decisions, and account exclusions explicit in the agreement.
