## The billing model changes the risk

A retainer charges for reserved capacity and work performed during a period. A pay-per-qualified-meeting model charges when an agreed meeting event happens. A hybrid model separates operating cost from the delivery fee.

None of these models makes a weak market work. The useful question is which risk each party controls.

## Compare the same outcome

Start with one written definition of a qualified held meeting. Use the same time period and include every mandatory charge.

For a retainer:

`cost per qualified held meeting = (setup + retainer + required extras) ÷ qualified held meetings`

Use parentheses in a spreadsheet:

`(setup + retainer + required extras) ÷ qualified held meetings`

For pay per meeting:

`cost = setup + operating cost + fee per meeting × qualified held meetings`

For a hybrid, use the same formula. The operating charge pays for infrastructure. The delivery fee pays for each accepted outcome.

## Check the edge cases

The headline price does not resolve billing disputes. The agreement must state what happens when:

- the prospect books but does not attend;
- the attendee leaves early;
- the company fits but the attendee lacks authority;
- the prospect already exists in the CRM;
- two people from one company attend;
- the buyer cancels or cannot take the call;
- the meeting is moved outside the billing period.

If the answer is missing, the effective price is still unknown.

## When a retainer can make sense

A retainer can fit a buyer who wants dedicated capacity, several channels, and work that matters even before meetings appear. It also gives the provider predictable capacity planning.

The buyer carries more delivery risk. The contract should therefore make the work, reporting, ownership, and exit terms explicit.

## When outcome billing can make sense

Outcome billing can fit a buyer who can define an acceptable meeting precisely and wants cost to move with delivery. The provider carries more risk between setup and the held meeting.

The buyer still funds infrastructure when the campaign requires domains, inboxes, data, and sending software. Calling that cost a delivery fee hides what it buys.
