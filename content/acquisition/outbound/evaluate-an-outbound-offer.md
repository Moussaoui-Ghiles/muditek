## Test the offer as a stranger

An offer that sells through referrals has not necessarily proved that it can sell cold. Review it without relying on existing trust.

Answer seven questions:

1. What specific problem does the buyer recognize?
2. What result changes after purchase?
3. What work will produce that result?
4. What relevant evidence supports the claim?
5. What money, time, disruption, or reputation can the buyer lose?
6. When does the first useful result appear?
7. What must the buyer commit before experiencing value?

## Check the commercial difference

Channel and delivery format are not the offer. A commercial difference must exist in the product, delivery, pricing, risk structure, or terms. A framework name is not a mechanism.

## Match claims to proof

Evidence must support the same buyer, problem, method, and result being promised. Narrow unsupported claims. Mark missing evidence as unknown.

## Choose the first commitment

A bounded first purchase can reduce commitment when it solves a real problem, produces a result the buyer can judge, states the full relationship honestly, and connects to the larger service.

The review produces a testable offer brief. It does not prove demand.
