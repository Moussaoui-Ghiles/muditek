## A signal changes a decision

A useful buyer signal changes account fit, priority, timing, or the reason for contact. It does not prove that a company wants to buy.

Evaluate each fact through four tests:

1. **Connection:** What does the fact establish about the problem, priority, or buying behavior?
2. **Reliability:** Can it be verified from a legitimate source?
3. **Recency:** When was it observed, and is it still relevant?
4. **Usability:** Does it change who you contact, when, or why?

## Separate fact from inference

Write the observable fact first. Then write the commercial inference. Keep them in separate fields.

"The company posted three compliance roles this month" is observable. "The company needs our service" is not established by that fact.

## Add a disqualifier

State what would make the signal irrelevant. This prevents researchers from approving every positive-looking event.

## Use honest personalization

Describe the observable situation. Ask whether the connected problem is relevant. Do not assert a private problem or buying plan that the evidence cannot prove.
