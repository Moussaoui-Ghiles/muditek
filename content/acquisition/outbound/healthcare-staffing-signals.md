## Use public facts to prioritize research

Healthcare staffing outreach can use public facility and workforce data to narrow a market. Public records do not prove an open requisition, staffing shortage, or intent to use an agency.

Useful research fields may include:

- facility type and location;
- ownership;
- certified bed count or service capacity;
- published staffing measures;
- turnover measures when the source provides them;
- current public job openings;
- observation date and source.

## Build the connection

State the facility fact, the operating question it raises, and the buyer role responsible for that decision. Keep the inference separate.

## Apply exclusions

Exclude facilities outside the service geography, facility types the client cannot support, records outside the freshness standard, and accounts without a verified relevant buyer path.

## Do not overstate the data

Ratings and staffing measures have defined source periods and methods. Preserve those labels. Use the evidence to prioritize account research, not to diagnose a private staffing need.
