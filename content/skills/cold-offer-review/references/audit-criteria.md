## Status discipline

Label each conclusion:

- `PASS`: the inspected evidence supports the narrow criterion and contains no material contradiction in scope.
- `FAIL`: inspected evidence shows an explicit mismatch, contradiction, hidden burden, or terms that defeat the stated offer.
- `UNPROVEN`: evidence is missing, inaccessible, stale for the claim, ambiguous, asserted without support, or not matched closely enough.

A `PASS` means only that the criterion is coherent and evidenced. It does not establish demand, urgency, product-market fit, offer validation, or likely conversion.

Determine the overall status for the exact decision under review:

- `FAIL` when any material evidenced contradiction or mismatch defeats that decision.
- `UNPROVEN` when no decisive failure is established but at least one material dependency remains unsupported.
- `PASS` only when the bounded coherence-and-evidence decision has no material `FAIL` or `UNPROVEN` remaining.

If an earlier link is `UNPROVEN` and a later link is a decisive `FAIL`, the overall decision is `FAIL`. Report the earliest unresolved link and the earliest evidenced broken link separately. Never use an overall `PASS` as a claim of market validation.

## Cold-context boundary

First classify the acquisition context:

- `true cold`: the buyer has no prior relationship or material trust exposure;
- `trust-assisted`: referral, partner, content, recognized brand or prior work transfers trust;
- `warm follow-up`: the buyer already engaged, applied, attended, subscribed, or entered a prior pipeline;
- `mixed` or `unknown`.

Do not use warm follow-up scripts, response rates, close rates, or trust as evidence that a true-cold offer works. Market size, customer value, buyer accessibility, opportunity source, and the full contacted-account-to-customer chain affect the motion; no source supplies a universal benchmark.

## Seven dimensions

### 1. Outcome

Test whether the intended buyer can identify the bounded business result, its scope, and the observable definition of done.

- `PASS`: the outcome, unit of result, boundary, and recognition method are explicit.
- `FAIL`: artifacts promise incompatible outcomes or the stated deliverables cannot produce the promised unit by their own terms.
- `UNPROVEN`: the outcome is vague, aspirational, or unsupported by an acceptance definition.

Do not assume an attached desire exists or market urgency is real. Require buyer evidence to establish those claims.

### 2. Problem

Audit two questions separately:

1. **Specificity and internal coherence:** does the current offer consistently name one buyer, situation, problem, consequence, and current alternative? Explicit scoped seller inputs can support a bounded `PASS` for this question.
2. **Buyer recognition and commercial importance:** do matched buyer records or first-party observations support recognition, prevalence, consequence, cost, or urgency? Seller assertions alone leave these claims `UNPROVEN`.

Use `FAIL` when inspected evidence contradicts the stated buyer or problem. Do not convert a coherent seller description into evidence that buyers recognize the problem or will act.

### 3. Method

Test whether the mechanism explains why the current alternative fails and how the offer produces a different result. Audit deliverables, sequence, ownership, dependencies, and acceptance.

- `PASS`: the causal path and delivery responsibilities are understandable and coherent.
- `FAIL`: the method conflicts with deliverables, relies on an unstated dependency, or is only a label masking a generic service.
- `UNPROVEN`: the causal explanation or operational path is absent.

### 4. Proof

Build a claim-to-proof map. Match buyer, problem, method, result, conditions, period, scope, acquisition motion, buyer visibility, and permission to use the evidence.

- `PASS`: each material claim is supported at the scope used and limitations are visible.
- `FAIL`: the offer presents a claim as established while the cited artifact explicitly concerns another buyer, method, period, or result, or the arithmetic contradicts the claim.
- `UNPROVEN`: proof is absent, self-reported without corroboration, stale, not buyer-visible, or too weakly matched.

Proof relevance and proof volume are separate. Never invent a minimum case-study count. A source author's result is not proof for the audited offer.

### 5. Risk

Map downside: money, time, data, reputation, implementation failure, opportunity cost, lock-in, and buyer obligations. Inspect risk reversal as written.

- `PASS`: operational risks, allocation, conditions, remedy, and buyer obligations are explicit and internally coherent.
- `FAIL`: the guarantee, performance term, or exit promise is contradicted by the terms, impossible to measure as written, or shifts undisclosed critical risk back to the buyer.
- `UNPROVEN`: material operational conditions, measurement, remedy, or capacity are unknown.

Report legal enforceability separately as `not assessed` or `unknown` unless current qualified evidence is in scope. Lack of legal review does not by itself fail otherwise clear operational risk terms. Do not invent a guarantee or provide legal advice.

### 6. Time to value

Separate onboarding, first useful output, first buyer-recognized value, promised outcome, and recurring value.

- `PASS`: the first-value event, prerequisites, expected timing, and owner are explicit.
- `FAIL`: timing claims conflict, prerequisites make the stated timing impossible on the offer's own schedule, or recurring payment lacks a stated recurring unit of value.
- `UNPROVEN`: a timeline exists but the first valuable event, dependencies, or evidence for timing does not.

### 7. First commitment

Identify the earliest meaningful ask across money, time, access, data, contract length, internal coordination, and change.

- **Call, meeting, or application:** audit purpose, expected outcome, qualification, buyer burden, transparency, and stated next step. It does not need to solve a standalone business problem.
- **Paid front-end offer, diagnostic, assessment, or pilot:** audit the same commitment factors and require a bounded useful result, a success definition, a deliverable the buyer can judge or keep, and a transparent connection to any larger service.
- **Direct purchase or contract:** audit the full commercial and operational commitment being requested; do not assume a smaller front end is required.

Use `FAIL` only for an evidenced material mismatch, such as hidden follow-on obligations or terms that contradict claimed reversibility or low commitment. Use `UNPROVEN` when the relevant purpose, burden, scope, exit, value, follow-on relationship, or success definition is unclear. Do not assume a pilot is low risk merely because it is called a pilot.

## Additional tests

Audit each separately with `PASS`, `FAIL`, or `UNPROVEN`:

### Buyer specificity

Can one role in one situation recognize that the offer is for them, including exclusions and disqualifiers?

### Cold-context clarity

Does the evidence distinguish true cold, trust-assisted, and warm follow-up performance? Does the offer rely on trust the cold buyer does not have?

### Channel and buying-context fit

Does the intended channel let the buyer understand and judge the offer at the commitment being requested? Keep channel reach, message performance, offer coherence, and sales execution separate. Do not infer that a coherent offer will work on email, LinkedIn, calling, or any other channel without comparable first-party evidence.

### Offer and terms coherence

Do website, proposal, call, pricing, payment, renewal, cancellation, guarantee, scope, acceptance, and dependency statements describe the same current offer?

### Positioning and packaging coherence

Do the product, category or positioning, current alternative, differentiator, package, and terms form one understandable commercial choice? Seller-defined positioning can pass for coherence; preference or superiority over the alternative requires relevant buyer evidence.

### Trust transfer

Identify the exact source of existing trust and whether the cold buyer receives it. A founder's audience, a referral, a partner introduction, a recognizable prior employer, and relevant delivery evidence transfer different things. None automatically proves the promised result.

### Claim-to-proof matching

For every material claim, test identity, relevance, recency, denominator, conditions, attribution, and buyer visibility. Narrow the conclusion to what the proof actually supports.

### Reversibility

Test exit rights, staged payments, data portability, switching cost, setup sunk cost, operational rollback, and whether the buyer can stop without losing essential assets or access.

### Buyer effort

List access, data cleanup, internal meetings, approvals, content, sales follow-up, training, adoption, and ongoing operations. Compare the disclosed burden with done-for-you or low-effort language.

### First-value integrity

For paid front-end offers, diagnostics, assessments, and pilots, test whether the commitment completes a bounded useful job with a success definition and an artifact or outcome the buyer can judge or keep. For a call, meeting, or application, test whether its purpose and expected outcome are clear and proportionate; do not require standalone delivery value.

### Delivery feasibility and scalability

Test whether the stated delivery can be repeated under the expected workload: provider labour, specialist capacity, bottlenecks, staffing, dependencies, marginal delivery cost, and failure conditions. Audit coherence against supplied operational evidence; do not import capacity or margin thresholds.

### Economics coherence

Audit formulas and assumptions, not market truth. Keep measured values separate from assumptions. Require period, source, numerator, denominator, capacity, margin, and attribution where relevant. Missing economics stay unknown, never zero.

## Earliest broken link

Trace in this order:

1. The buyer recognizes themselves and the sales context.
2. The buyer recognizes the problem and consequence.
3. The buyer can judge the desired outcome.
4. The buyer understands the method, deliverables, and effort.
5. The buyer can believe each material claim from matched proof or transferred trust.
6. The buyer can judge risk, timing, price, terms, and reversibility.
7. The buyer can accept the stated first commitment with clear purpose, burden, terms, and expected outcome; a paid front end also creates a bounded useful result.

Name the first `FAIL` whose preceding links are not failed. If an earlier link is `UNPROVEN`, report both the evidenced failure and the earlier unresolved dependency. If no link fails, name the earliest `UNPROVEN`. Never describe `UNPROVEN` as broken.

## Source grounding and limits

The framework uses documented operator material about offer construction, cold versus warm context, commercial differentiation, risk, first commitment, and outbound motion. These sources provide context, not universal evidence or thresholds.

Treat all operator numbers, conversion claims, case-study counts, channel claims, pricing examples, guarantees, and offer preferences as operator assertions or self-reported examples. Do not convert them into pass criteria, thresholds, buyer facts, legal advice, or proof of likely performance.

Offer and intake documents can supply reusable input categories such as buyer, current state, trigger, desired outcome, systems, process, price, sales cycle, access, concerns, failure definition, and constraints. They remain inputs, not public proof or validation standards.
