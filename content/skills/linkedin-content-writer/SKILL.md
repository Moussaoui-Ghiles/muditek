# LinkedIn content writer

Input: one complete source or draft.

Output: one paste-ready LinkedIn post under 3,000 characters.

Follow every step in order. Move forward only when the gate passes.

## 1. Read

Read the complete input and both references:

- [Halbert headlines](references/halbert-headlines.md)
- [LinkedIn winners](references/linkedin-winners.md)

Gate: the input and both references have been read in full.

## 2. Lock the source

Identify the source's exact topic, main claim or result, evidence, examples, sequence, uncertainty, and attribution. The source owns all of them.

The user is always the author, owner, and narrator of the finished post. Name no other person, source creator, or author unless the user explicitly requests it.

Gate: every planned factual claim maps to the input.

## 3. Choose the post structure

Choose the structure already present in the source:

- list for themed items in flexible order;
- steps for actions that require an order to produce a result;
- story for events that lead to a resolution or lesson;
- argument for a claim supported by evidence and reasoning;
- case breakdown for a result explained by its situation and evidence;
- comparison for differences that affect a decision;
- news explanation for a change, its evidence, and its consequence.

Argument, case, comparison, and news are LinkedIn editorial forms. Carry their attention through Hormozi's lists, steps, stories, or a useful combination.

Gate: the structure preserves the source's real order and can fit below 3,000 characters without breaking its promise.

## 4. Hook attention

Hormozi defines the hook as topic + headline + format. The input supplies the topic. LinkedIn supplies the format. Build the headline from the source.

### Halbert headline pass

1. Review the worked headline constructions in the Halbert reference.
2. Name the exact event, result, problem, mechanism, or claim in the source.
3. Find the strongest truthful news, benefit, or news-plus-benefit already in the source.
4. Add source-backed specificity: numbers, timeframes, mechanisms, events, or visible results. Add a person's name only when the user explicitly requests it.
5. Draft genuinely different directions. Use the Halbert constructions only when the source supports them.
6. Return to what is true and relevant. Reject playful practice lines that distort the source.
7. Compare the strongest alternatives phrase by phrase. Keep only phrases that add attention, clarity, credibility, benefit, specificity, consequence, or curiosity.
8. Select the headline that gives the clearest truthful reason to consume and that the body can fulfil.

### Hormozi headline pass

Strengthen the headline with news components already present in the source:

- recency;
- relevance to the reader;
- a person or authority the user explicitly requested to name;
- proximity to the reader's place or situation;
- conflict;
- unusualness;
- an ongoing development.

Use one or more real components. Try two when they fit naturally. The components are ingredients, not a template.

### Opening bridge

Follow the headline with source-backed context, proof, tension, a question, a story already in motion, or a concrete picture. The bridge must make the expected reward clear and lead directly into the body.

Gate: the opening states the exact subject, gives a truthful reason to continue, and creates a promise the body can fulfil.

## 5. Retain attention

Hormozi's retention mechanism is curiosity through an unresolved question, explicit or implicit. Carry that question with the structure that fits the source.

### Lists

- Keep one clear theme.
- State the exact count in the headline or opening when the count is part of the hook.
- Let the remaining items create the unresolved question.
- Deliver every promised item.

### Steps

- Use steps only when order matters.
- State the result the sequence produces.
- Make each early step clear and useful so the reader wants the remaining steps.
- Preserve every dependency needed to reach the result.

### Stories

- Start inside a real source event or establish the situation quickly.
- Preserve what happened, what changed, and what remained unresolved.
- Move through events toward the answer.
- End with the supported resolution or lesson.

Lists, steps, and stories can interweave when each part adds clarity or curiosity. Longer posts can link smaller Hook-Retain-Reward units inside sections.

Gate: every paragraph advances an unresolved question with a fact, step, example, reason, event, consequence, or useful distinction from the source.

## 6. Reward attention

Reward means satisfying the reason the reader started.

- Answer every unresolved question.
- Deliver the promised quantity and quality.
- Keep the material relevant and usable for the people the hook addresses.
- Increase value per second: each section must provide a useful fact, example, explanation, distinction, or action.
- Match or exceed the expectation created by the opening.

If the source cannot fit under 3,000 characters, write one complete content unit around the source's main topic. Make a promise that fits that unit. Do not claim to cover the entire source.

Gate: removing any remaining paragraph would remove needed proof, meaning, progression, or reward.

## 7. Unslop the draft

Use plain words, concrete nouns, active verbs, and natural paragraph rhythm. Remove filler, inflated claims, generic transitions, repeated conclusions, forced symmetry, empty suspense, and abstract labels that add no meaning.

The unslop pass changes wording only. It preserves the source facts, hook promise, structure, and meaning.

Gate: every sentence adds information, movement, proof, or payoff.

## 8. Verify

Give the source and draft to an independent factual verifier when available. It checks:

- topic fidelity;
- unsupported or changed claims;
- changed scope, conditions, uncertainty, sequence, causation, or metric meaning;
- the selected retention structure;
- unfulfilled opening questions or promises.

Correct every failure and recheck. If no verifier is available, perform the same check line by line.

Gate: zero source-fidelity failures and zero unfulfilled promises remain.

## 9. Format and return

- Opening block first.
- Short readable paragraphs.
- Numbers for ordered actions.
- Bullets for unordered items.
- CTA after the reward when requested.
- P.S. only for an additional link, resource, or action.

Return only the finished post.
