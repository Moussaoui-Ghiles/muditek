# Newsletter

Write a long-form issue that gets finished and leaves the reader able to understand, decide, diagnose, or do something useful.

Every sentence must create clarity. If the reader must assemble, decode, or reread it, rewrite it.

Read:

- `references/structure.md` for beat order and closing mechanics;
- `references/language.md` for sentence and formatting rules.

These files control how the issue is written. The input controls what the issue says.

## Required brief

Establish before drafting:

1. **Source set:** the complete notes, article, transcript, data, or artifacts.
2. **Reader:** the specific person and the problem they already recognize.
3. **Promise:** the one result the issue will deliver.
4. **Argument:** the source-backed explanation that fulfils the promise.
5. **Content job:** recognition, education, demonstration, trust, or conversion.
6. **Close:** no CTA, a reader action, a matched asset, or an approved commercial next step.

If the reader, problem, promise, and argument do not fit together, return one proposed brief and wait. Do not draft around the gap.

## 1. Check input fitness

For each input, identify the claim, mechanism, example, or question it supports.

- Use material that supports the approved argument.
- Exclude material that answers a different question.
- Preserve a real disagreement or resolve it with evidence.
- Do not blend conflicting claims into a false consensus.

Name any material exclusion that changes the user's intended piece.

## 2. Protect the proof boundary

Use a first-person claim only when it comes from the current task or an approved first-party artifact.

Keep these states separate: planned, contracted, paid, built, tested, deployed, adopted, and produced a business result.

Do not invent:

- a client or quotation;
- a personal experience;
- a result, revenue figure, or sample size;
- a causal explanation;
- texture added to make a story feel real.

Attribute consequential external evidence. Do not make an external result read like the author's result.

## 3. Choose one reader state

Identify whether the reader is:

- unaware;
- problem aware;
- solution aware;
- product aware;
- ready to decide.

Start where that reader is. Do not explain a problem to somebody already living it. A solution-aware reader often needs to understand why earlier attempts failed and what mechanism changes the result.

## 4. Lock one idea

Write the issue's single idea in one sentence.

A diagnosis and its fix can be one idea. Two unrelated payoffs are two issues. Park one.

The idea must represent the source set. Do not select a convenient subtopic simply because it is easier to write.

## 5. Fill the evidence slots

Answer:

1. What does the reader want?
2. What have they tried?
3. Why did it fail?
4. Why does this mechanism work differently?
5. What proof supports that explanation?
6. What can the reader do after reading?

Research only the empty slots when the user permits research. Do not replace supplied facts with more dramatic web examples.

## 6. Write the title

The title selects the right reader and states one complete promise.

Use one complete sentence. Prefer a concrete fact, problem, mechanism, or result over a clever label. Do not stack fragments. Do not make the reader decode an invented concept.

Read the title once as a stranger. If the payoff is unclear, rewrite it.

## 7. Choose the shape

Use one shape from `references/structure.md`:

- **Essay:** one argument developed through evidence, consequence, and judgment.
- **Guide:** one result delivered through ordered, usable steps.

Do not mix the shapes. Structure first. Sentence polish comes second.

## 8. Draft and verify

Write the issue, then check:

- the title and body deliver the same promise;
- every example and number is sourced;
- every paragraph advances the argument or procedure;
- the vocabulary stays consistent;
- the close follows delivered value;
- any CTA is approved and relevant;
- nothing claims publication or delivery.

Remove generic introductions, repeated conclusions, unsupported urgency, and sales-page agitation.

## Output

Return the finished issue. Do not publish, send, subscribe anyone, or claim the issue is live.
