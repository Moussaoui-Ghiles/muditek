# Source Boundaries

## Purpose

Separate sourced operator lessons from transparent definitions created for this diagnostic. Do not imply that a practitioner supplied every formula or that a self-reported example is a benchmark.

## Public source set

### Final outbound diagnostic article

The final article establishes the calculator’s scope and operating promise:

- preserve channel-specific denominators;
- use unique prospects for cold email and LinkedIn and unique accounts for named-account outreach;
- return unknown for missing inputs and not applicable for explicit zero denominators;
- connect funnel stages to fully loaded acquisition cost and customer gross profit;
- calculate required pilot performance instead of inventing conversion rates;
- use rates to select what to investigate, not to claim causation;
- keep AI inside evidence organization, calculation, comparison, and exception handling.

Treat this as the product specification for the skill, not external validation.

### Cole Gordon: marginal economics

Original: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8lC4nhA8UY

Use:

- compare the additional benefit of the next unit with its additional cost;
- diminishing marginal return means averages can conceal weaker next-unit economics;
- improve the system when additional cost rises faster than additional value.

Treat this as an operator framework. Do not present its anecdotes or company figures as independently verified evidence.

### Daniel Fazio: constraint investigation

Original: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KgUEurlgXCQ

Use:

- investigate the current constraint instead of changing several unrelated parts at once;
- a visible top-line outcome can conceal a different operational bottleneck.

Do not import the example’s prices, close rate, calendar capacity, or recommended staffing as a benchmark.

## Transparent diagnostic definitions

The formulas in `metric-specification.md` are arithmetic definitions within this outbound diagnostic. Their value comes from explicit units, cost boundaries, horizons, and evidence states. They are not claimed as quotations or proprietary formulas from the operator sources.

This distinction is essential:

- `customers / entries` is a declared cohort-yield definition;
- `cost / customers` is the declared CAC definition for the same attributed cohort;
- `collected revenue - direct delivery cost` is the declared gross-profit basis;
- `ceiling(cost / contribution)` is transparent break-even arithmetic;
- the first month where cumulative gross profit reaches CAC is the declared payback rule.
- `cohort cost / unique entities reaching a stage` is a declared average stage-cost definition, not marginal cost;
- `first-year gross profit / CAC` is a declared coverage multiple, not LTV, ROI, or cash payback;
- `first-year gross profit * customers - acquisition cost` is a declared cohort contribution view, not net profit.

Never add an external “healthy” threshold unless the user supplies a current governing target or explicitly requests authoritative benchmark research. Label any company target as a decision rule and preserve its source.
