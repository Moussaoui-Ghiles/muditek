# Hook review

The hook is the topic, first-line promise, and native format working together.

## Find candidates

Read the complete source. Pull true candidates from:

- a named launch, change, tool, company, or regulation;
- a measured result;
- a useful before-and-after state;
- a clear procedure or deliverable;
- a source-backed disagreement;
- a concrete problem with a real consequence;
- an artifact the reader can use.

## Select one

Prefer the candidate that:

- names the subject early;
- is specific without losing context;
- matters to the intended reader;
- creates a promise the body can fulfil;
- does not need invented suspense.

Specific does not mean louder. A real mechanism is stronger than an adjective. A precise condition is stronger than a universal claim.

## Reject the hook when

- it could introduce almost any topic;
- it hides the subject behind pronouns or abstractions;
- it adds a number, deadline, or result that the source does not contain;
- it claims “nobody,” “everyone,” or “most people” without evidence;
- it creates a conflict the body never proves;
- it promises a complete guide but the body delivers a partial note;
- it reads like three clipped fragments the reader must assemble;
- it depends on unexplained context from an earlier post.

## Final test

A stranger should understand the subject, the reason to continue, and the expected payoff after one read. The last line must satisfy the expectation created by the first.
