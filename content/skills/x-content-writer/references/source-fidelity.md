# Source fidelity

Faithful writing has two requirements:

1. Every fact, number, name, condition, and result traces to the supplied source or an explicitly approved proof set.
2. The prose is newly written for X. It is not a sentence-by-sentence copy of the source.

## Preserve exactly

- who did the work;
- what was built, changed, observed, or measured;
- the metric definition and time period;
- the sequence of events or steps;
- causation versus correlation;
- uncertainty, limitations, and exceptions;
- whether a state is planned, built, tested, deployed, adopted, or proven by a business result;
- attribution and permission boundaries.

Do not round a number for punch. Do not upgrade a test into a deployment, a deployment into adoption, or a booking into a held meeting.

## Shape, do not reframe

You may:

- move the sharpest true fact to the opening;
- compress repeated explanation;
- choose a list, sequence, case, comparison, or argument structure;
- rewrite sentences in a clearer native style;
- remove material that does not support the post's promise.

You may not:

- invent an organizing thesis;
- claim first-person experience that the source does not contain;
- create an enemy or industry consensus;
- add stakes, urgency, scarcity, or inevitability;
- convert an external example into the user's result;
- make an estimate look measured;
- turn an anecdote into a general rule.

If a useful claim cannot be traced, omit it or ask for evidence.

## Line-by-line check

For each sentence, identify:

- its source fact;
- whether its scope matches;
- whether the level of certainty matches;
- whether the attribution is clear;
- whether it is needed to fulfil the promise.

A sentence fails if any answer is missing.
